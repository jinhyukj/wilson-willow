On love, labour & time

The Tender Hours

By Wilson Willow

Where men must sell their days for bread And bend beneath another’s will, The tender hours of love are fled, And all the heart’s desires lie still.

For love must have its time to bloom, Its quiet walk, its evening light; But labour follows to the room And takes the remnant of the night.

And those who live by others’ toil, With idle hours enough to spare, Have learned to count their golden spoil, But long forgotten how to share.
